Phase 1: Line-by-Line Autopsy Every Phrase Dissected

"industry-leading, next-generation"
Unverified Boast
What is wrong: The self-coronation trap. You founded the company sixteen months ago. You have fourteen employees and a staging database running on unpaid cloud credits. You are not leading an industry. You barely lead your cap table. Stop that. "Next-generation" tells the reader nothing about your architecture.
Why it hurts you: Enterprise buyers read "industry-leading" as shorthand for "we have no verifiable customer references to show you." They smirk and close the tab.
Surgical Fix:
"Deployed on dedicated clusters with sub-100ms response times as of Q1 2026."
"empowers modern revenue teams"
Semantic Bankruptcy
What is wrong: "Empowers" is a corporate non-verb used when you cannot name the physical task your software performs. "Modern revenue teams" is lazy demographic pandering. What makes them modern? They use Slack and drink cold brew?
Why it hurts you: VPs of Sales and RevOps Directors do not buy empowerment. They buy saved labor hours, lower rep attrition, plus error-free pipeline tracking. If you cannot name their daily pain, they know you cannot solve it.
Surgical Fix:
"Cuts daily CRM update time for mid-market account executives from 45 minutes to 3 minutes."
"seam-lessly un-lock autonomous agentic intelligence"
Smoke Screen
What is wrong: A linguistic pile-up. "Seam-lessly" is the biggest lie in B2B software: every enterprise deployment involves broken authentication, firewall policies, API rate limits, and permission headaches. It is five syllables of technical smoke screen for an asynchronous loop calling a model endpoint.
Why it hurts you: Your buyer's VP of Engineering reads this and anticipates three months of custom middleware development followed by an outage. It triggers immediate technical skepticism.
Surgical Fix:
"Triggers background routines that query your database, draft outbound follow-ups, and flag discrepancies without manual data entry."
"enterprise-grade, end-to-end solution"
Checklist Defense
What is wrong: "Enterprise-grade" usually means you finally turned on SAML single sign-on last Tuesday and bought a basic cyber insurance policy. "End-to-end solution" is an admission that you do not have a sharp wedge product, so you pretend to boil the entire ocean.
Why it hurts you: Enterprise procurement teams know true enterprise software takes months to certify. Calling yourself "end-to-end" signals that your tool is shallow across every single feature.
Surgical Fix:
"SOC 2 Type II certified. Encrypted at rest via AES-256. Covers the intake and direct CRM logging steps of inbound deal flow."
"drive scalable hyper-growth and maximize pipeline velocity"
Boardroom Cosplay
What is wrong: Software does not drive hyper-growth. Product-market fit, unit economics, and sales execution drive growth. Software is plumbing. "Maximize pipeline velocity" sounds like an excerpt from an unread textbook.
Why it hurts you: Chief Commercial Officers hear this line twenty times a week. It registers as white noise. It proves the vendor does not understand how deals actually stall in legal and security reviews.
Surgical Fix:
"Shortens average sales cycle duration by eliminating manual quoting delays and automating approval handoffs."
"harnessing the power of frontier generative models"
API Markup
What is wrong: Third-party rent-seeking disguised as proprietary deep tech. You bought API tokens from OpenAI or Anthropic. You put a system prompt in a repository. You did not harness an elemental force. You are an API consumer like everyone else.
Why it hurts you: Smart buyers realize they are paying you a 400% markup on top of raw inference costs. They begin calculating the cost of having an internal junior engineer build your product over a weekend.
Surgical Fix:
"Runs on fine-tuned Llama-3-70B weights hosted on private VPCs, with automatic fallback routing to Claude 3.5 Sonnet during latency spikes."
"transform complex conversational chaos into actionable business outcomes"
Purple Prose
What is wrong: "Conversational chaos" is a dramatic metaphor for unorganized sales call recordings. "Actionable business outcomes" is an abstraction hiding database rows.
Why it hurts you: Buyers do not think in metaphors. They think in deliverables. When they read poetic fluff, they assume the software produces low-accuracy hallucinations that need human supervision anyway.
Surgical Fix:
"Transcribes customer calls, extracts customer budget limits and renewal dates, and writes them into Salesforce custom fields."
"trusted by Fortune 500 innovators to revolutionize how modern enterprises win"
Phantom Social Proof
What is wrong: Which Fortune 500 company? Which department? A rogue tester on a corporate card does not mean the Fortune 500 trusts you. "Revolutionize how modern enterprises win" is empty noise.
Why it hurts you: Enterprise buyers check references. When you hide behind anonymous collective nouns like "innovators", everyone knows you do not have signed master service agreements with verified reference accounts.
Surgical Fix:
"Currently processing over 120,000 monthly sales interactions across eight enterprise pilots in healthcare and financial services."

Hollow Phrase Detected Why It Failed Replace With This Instead
Industry-leading Unearned self-praise. Signals an insecure startup. Exact market share, ARR milestone, or benchmark rank.
Next-generation Chronological fluff explaining zero architecture. Deployment model or exact specs.
Empowers Passive HR speak. The software does not give authority. Concrete action verbs: calculates, logs, transcribes.
Seam-lessly Every integration has friction. Claiming otherwise is a lie. Actual setup time: Connects via REST API in under 20 minutes.
Un-lock Mystical marketing trope. You are not picking a lock. Direct access nouns: exposes, indexes, queries.
Autonomous agentic intelligence Pseudoscience jargon covering up basic code execution. Exact mechanics: Background scripts triggered by webhooks.
Enterprise-grade Meaningless without certifications. Exact compliance badges: SOC 2, HIPAA, ISO 27001, SAML.
End-to-end solution Claims to do everything, usually does nothing well. Specific entry and exit points of your data pipeline.
Hyper-growth VC pitch deck bait. Customers do not care about valuation. The customer operational metric: Cuts CAC by 18%.
Pipeline velocity Abstract jargon masking lack of measurable sales impact. Days from discovery call to signed contract.
Harnessing the power of Flamboyant filler for calling someone else API. Queries Anthropic and OpenAI foundational models.
Actionable business outcomes Bureaucratic smoke screen for structured output. The actual data asset: Clean JSON rows written to your CRM.
